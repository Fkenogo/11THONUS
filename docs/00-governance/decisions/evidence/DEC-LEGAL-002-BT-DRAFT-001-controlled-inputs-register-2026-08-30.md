- The Business-verification discretion clause (§8.7) is drafted from LEG-FD-01's general fallback interpretive standard (transparency, fairness, proportionality) precisely because no more specific verification SLA, criterion set, or automatic-approval rule exists in any reviewed authority — this is treated as a case where the general standard is sufficient to draft a discretion clause, not as a gap requiring a new controlled input, consistent with the Terms Drafting Readiness Note's own "Ready" classification for this readiness-table row (`ENG-P3-002` onboarding architecture).

# Part III review (task `DEC-LEGAL-002-BT-DRAFT-003`)

Part III (§§11–14) was drafted entirely on principle-based language already supported by existing authority (FD-2, FD-3, FD-5, FD-6; `DEC-LOY-011` CONFIRMED; LEG-FD-01, LEG-FD-04, LEG-FD-05, LEG-FD-07, LEG-FD-08; `TRD10-firestore-data-architecture.md` §§10.9–10.12; `TRD17-subscription-and-billing.md` §§17.19–17.20; Part I §§4/6 and Part II §10 of this same instrument, cross-referenced not redrafted). No `[CONTROLLED INPUT REQUIRED: ...]` marker was needed in Part III, and no new controlled input is added to this register by this task. In particular:

- **`DEC-LOY-009` is not resolved, and no new controlled input is created for it.** `DEC-LOY-009` ("Reward quantity default and >1 support") remains `OPEN_FOUNDER` in the Decision Register, unchanged. Two specific propositions — that reward quantity is fixed at creation, and that multiple unredeemed rewards may coexist under a Reward Program — were identified by prior legal-preparation work as unsupported by any governed authority while `DEC-LOY-009` remains open, and were accordingly withdrawn from consideration rather than drafted. §13.7 of the instrument makes this boundary explicit in the contractual text itself (an affirmative statement of non-resolution, not a proposition requiring its own controlled-input marker), so that no reader could infer either proposition from §13's silence on quantity or coexistence. This is a drafting-discipline safeguard, not a gap requiring Founder or legal input before Part III can be approved on its own terms — §13 is fully draftable, and was drafted, without resolving `DEC-LOY-009`. `DEC-LOY-009` remains outside this register because it is not a Business-Terms-drafting input at all; it is a Reward Program schema/product decision (`rewardQuantity` field default) that this instrument does not need resolved to state the obligation-survival, suspension, exit, value, and programme-change principles that make up §13 and §14.
- **The reward-expiry clause (§13.6) is drafted from `DEC-LOY-005` (CONFIRMED), not from the schema alone — the original rationale below is superseded, see the correction report.** ~~The reward-expiry clause (§13.6) is drafted from the `rewards.status` schema's `"expired"` value (`TRD10-firestore-data-architecture.md` §10.12.1) precisely because no more specific platform-wide expiry duration, minimum/maximum, or trigger rule exists in any reviewed authority — this is treated as a case where stating that expiry (where present) is a Reward-Program-level matter, subject to the earned-reward-survival principle (§13.4) and applicable law, is sufficient to draft a boundary-respecting clause, not as a gap requiring a new controlled input.~~ **Corrected on PR #205 review:** the schema's `"expired"` state existing does not, by itself, authorize a Business to rely on expiry — `decision-register.md`'s `DEC-LOY-005` ("No automatic reward expiry in MVP") is CONFIRMED and states earned rewards do not currently auto-expire, with the `expired` state architecturally supported but not enabled. §13.6 is now drafted directly from `DEC-LOY-005`: no current auto-expiry, this clause does not itself authorize a Business's Reward Program to provide for expiry, and any future per-program expiry policy requires separate governed platform authorization. This is not treated as a new controlled input — `DEC-LOY-005` already resolves the underlying question; §13.6 states that resolution rather than inventing one.
- The suspension-redemption clause (§13.2) and the exit clause (§13.3) are drafted from `DEC-LOY-011` (CONFIRMED) and FD-2/FD-3/LEG-FD-06/07/08 precisely because those authorities already resolve the product-policy questions those clauses state (default-redeemable-with-exceptions; no universal run-off; no mandatory cash settlement) — the only matters those authorities leave open (the exception/manual-review workflow design; the suspension grounds/process detail; the exit run-off/transition mechanism) are expressly reserved to undrafted Part IV §§15–16, not drafted or approximated here, consistent with the task's cross-reference-only instruction.
- The programme-change notice clause (§14.3) is drafted from LEG-FD-05's reasonable-notice standard precisely because no fixed platform-wide notice period is governed (the Legal Opinion's 30-day recommendation was expressly not adopted) — stating a reasonable-advance-communication standard without a fixed period is the sufficient, non-inventive drafting choice, not a gap requiring a new controlled input.
- **§11.1 (earning threshold and qualifying-purchase authority) is corrected twice, across rounds 5 and 6, and creates no new controlled input.** Round 5: the original wording stated the Business determines "the threshold(s) for earning a reward" and "what qualifies as a rewardable purchase." The threshold point was a genuine gap — `TRD10-firestore-data-architecture.md` §10.9.2's Threshold Rule (MVP) fixes `requiredVerifiedUnits` at 10 by platform rule, expressly "not business-configurable" — corrected so the Business's authority does not extend to a platform-fixed threshold (also corrected in §11.6, round 6, which had separately restated the threshold as Business-controlled). On the qualifying-purchase point, round 5's first correction added a disclaimer that the "qualifying purchase" question is separately open (per the Legal Counsel Handoff Pack §4) — but round 6 review correctly identified that this created an apparent internal contradiction with the already-approved Part I §6.2, which allocates "what qualifies as a rewardable purchase" to the Business as part of its Reward Program responsibility as existing, unreopened Founder-approved baseline. Per the governing task instruction not to substantively rewrite §§1–10, §6.2 was not touched; instead §11.1 was corrected to cross-reference §6.2's existing allocation directly, removing the disclaimer that risked appearing to contradict it. This is not a new controlled input: the Business's Reward-Program-level qualifying-purchase authority was already established by the Founder-approved Part I baseline before this task began; this task only corrected Part III's phrasing to state that existing allocation consistently rather than independently re-deciding or appearing to reopen it. The distinct, broader "qualifying-purchase definition" item the Legal Counsel Handoff Pack §4 lists as genuinely open (a legal/regulatory-level question, not a Reward-Program-design question) remains untouched by either §6.2 or §11.1 and is not resolved by this instrument.
- **§14.4 (customer-facing Reward Program reacceptance) is drafted as an explicit non-resolution, not a new controlled input.** The original PR #205 wording asserted that no customer reacceptance is required for a Reward Program change — a PR #205 Codex review finding correctly identified this as prematurely selecting a policy on a question the Legal Counsel Handoff Pack §4 records as genuinely open ("programme publication (does publication create a binding offer to customers?)", expressly not asked of counsel as if decided) and that Part I §7.4 (which concerns a Business reaccepting these platform Terms) does not support extending to how a Business's customers accept the Business's own programme changes. The clause was corrected to state that this question is neither required nor foreclosed by this section — the same explicit-non-resolution technique §13.7 uses for `DEC-LOY-009`. This does not create a new controlled input: no Founder or legal action is required before Part III itself can be approved, because §14 does not depend on the answer to state the prospective-only/no-retrospective-reduction/reasonable-notice principles that are §14's actual subject.

# Classification key

- **Required before Founder approval** — the Founder must supply a product/commercial position before this clause can be finalized, independent of legal review.
- **Required before legal approval** — legal counsel must supply an opinion or confirm sufficiency before this clause can be finalized, independent of Founder input.
- **Required before Terms configuration** — the input is not needed to finalize clause wording, but must exist before a Terms version referencing this clause can be assigned/configured in `platformConfig/businessTerms`.
- **Jurisdiction-overlay input** — the input is expected to be supplied differently per jurisdiction, via the overlay mechanism (§26 of the draft), not as a single global value.
- **Future commercial input** — the input depends on an unresolved `DEC-SUB-*` or other commercial decision not yet reached.

# Flagged pre-existing defect — NOT resolved by this task, NOT a new controlled input, Part I §4.2 (discovered during PR #205 review round 7)

During PR #205 review round 7, a genuine factual conflict was discovered in **already-approved Part I §4.2** (drafted in task `DEC-LEGAL-002-BT-DRAFT-001`, Founder-approved via `-CORR-001`, unedited by Parts II or III):

> "4.2 ... 11thONUS ... does not itself decide what a Business's Reward Program rewards, at what threshold, or with what value. Those are decisions the Business makes for its own Reward Program (§6)."

This directly conflicts with `TRD10-firestore-data-architecture.md` §10.9.2's Threshold Rule (MVP): `requiredVerifiedUnits` "is stored as a number for architectural consistency, but for the MVP it is **fixed at 10 by platform rule and is not business-configurable**... Alternative thresholds may be introduced only through formal product approval and never retroactively." The earning threshold is not, in fact, a decision the Business makes — it is a platform-fixed MVP rule.

This defect **predates this task entirely** — it was present in the original Part I draft (`DEC-LEGAL-002-BT-DRAFT-001`) and was not caught by that task's own authority review, nor by either of its correction passes, nor by Part II's drafting or its two correction passes. It surfaced only because Part III's own §11.1/§11.6 correctly stated the platform-fixed-threshold rule (per PR #205 review rounds 5–6), creating a visible internal contradiction with Part I.

**Per the governing task instruction (§16: "If a substantive change to §§1–10 appears necessary, STOP and report it for Founder review"), Part I §4.2 is NOT edited by this task.** It is instead flagged here as a discovered defect requiring Founder/legal correction in a future task, following the same instrument-integrity discipline this task applied to §6.2 (round 6): a substantive change to Part I is reported, not silently made.

**Classification:**
- **Does not block Part III's own approval** — Part III's clauses (§11.1, §11.6) independently and correctly state the platform-fixed threshold; the defect exists entirely within Part I's own text.
- **Blocks whole-instrument (Parts I–III, and ultimately I–VIII) Founder approval as a single coherent document** — the instrument currently contains an internal factual contradiction about who controls the earning threshold (Part I §4.2 says the Business; Part III §§11.1/11.6 say the platform, correctly) that must be reconciled before the full document is approved as internally consistent.
- **Does not block Terms configuration or implementation** — no Terms version is configured regardless; this is a drafting-consistency defect, not an engineering blocker.

**Recommended resolution (not performed here):** correct Part I §4.2 to remove "at what threshold" from the list of Business-made decisions (or to state the threshold is platform-fixed, consistent with Part III), via a dedicated Founder-authorized correction task to Part I, following the same pattern already used for `DEC-LEGAL-002-BT-DRAFT-001-CORR-001`.
