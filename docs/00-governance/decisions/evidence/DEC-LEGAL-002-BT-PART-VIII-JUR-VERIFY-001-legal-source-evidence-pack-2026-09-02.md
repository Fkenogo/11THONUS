## 2. Burundi — Law No. 1/22 of 22 August 2024 (Code des Communications Electroniques et Postales)

**Status: DIRECT PRIMARY-TEXT VERIFIED** (expanded under CORR-001 to the substantive chapter)

- **Jurisdiction:** Burundi
- **Official title:** Loi N deg 1/22 du 22 aout 2024 portant Code des Communications Electroniques et Postales
- **Issuing authority / source:** Republic of Burundi, Cabinet du President; published by ARCT (Agence de Regulation et de Controle des Telecommunications)
- **Source URL:** `https://arct.gov.bi/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/LOI_COMMUNICATIONS-ELECTRONIQUES.pdf`
- **Relevant articles read directly:** Titre I, Chapitres I-II (Art. 1-3: object, scope, definitions, pp. 1-11); **Chapitre VII: Des Transactions par Voie Electronique** (Art. 137-146, pp. 32-34) — Section 1 "De la reconnaissance de la valeur juridique des messages de donnees" (Art. 137-138), Section 2 "De la formation des contrats sous forme electronique" (Art. 139-142), Section 3 "Du commerce par voie electronique" (Art. 143-146).
- **Concise verified legal propositions:**
  - Art. 137: an advanced-signature-plus-qualified-certificate standard applies only where another law formally requires a signature for the specific act; an ordinary electronic signature otherwise cannot be denied legal value merely for being electronic.
  - Art. 138: a data message is admissible evidence, equal to paper writing, with probative force assessed by reliability of creation/conservation/communication and identification of the sender.
  - Art. 139-140: contract/communication validity and consent are not deniable solely for electronic form.
  - Art. 141: receipt acknowledgment by party agreement or express acknowledgment.
  - Art. 142: retrievability duty — expressly addressed to "un consommateur" (consumer-facing, not B2B).
  - Art. 143: e-commerce activity governed by the law of the state of establishment, subject to parties' common intent as to destination.
  - Art. 144: operator-identity/registration/VAT/authorization disclosures apply to "toute personne qui exerce une activite de commerce par voie electronique" — consistent with the existing row-11 A classification.
  - Art. 145-146: pre-contract information duties expressly addressed to consumers.
- **Matrix row affected:** Row 3 (Burundi e-contracting). Result: **D -> C**, primary-text-verified.
- **Residual uncertainty:** No exhaustive search confirmed that no other Burundi statute imposes a signature formality on this specific B2B instrument type; the 2026 Personal Data Protection Law's "demonstrable consent" language remains uncross-checked against Law 1/22's later chapters (not reached within this task's budget).

## 3. Rwanda — Law N deg 45/2011 of 25/11/2011 Governing Contracts

**Status: DIRECT PRIMARY-TEXT VERIFIED** (corrected under CORR-001 — originally SECONDARY / SUPPORTING only via Chambers and Partners; now read directly)

- **Jurisdiction:** Rwanda
- **Official title:** Law N deg 45/2011 of 25/11/2011 Governing Contracts
- **Issuing authority / source:** Parliament of the Republic of Rwanda; Official Gazette n deg 04bis of 23/01/2012
- **Source URL:** `https://www.minecofin.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minecofin/Publications/LAWS/Other_laws/LAW__NO_45.2011_OF_25.11.2011_GOVERNING__CONTRACTS_IN_RWANDA_.pdf`
- **Relevant articles read directly:** Art. 58-61 (Chapter III, Section 4, "Non-performance of the contract on grounds of public order"); Art. 65 (good faith); Art. 91 (factors extinguishing the obligation to pay damages on repudiation); Art. 92-97 (Section 2, "Impossibility of performance of the contract")
- **Concise verified legal propositions:**
  - Art. 58: "A promise or other clause of a contract is unenforceable on the grounds of public order if the Law provides that it cannot be performed or depending on the circumstances, the public order prevails over the interest in the performance of a contract."
  - Art. 59-61: a **case-specific balancing test** — parties' expectations and prejudice/public interest in performance (59); weight of the relevant public-order rule, severity/deliberateness of misconduct, and its connection to the clause (60); the bases on which a public-order motive can obstruct performance (61).
  - Art. 91: extinguishes the obligation to pay damages **on repudiation**, where the repudiated obligations would themselves have been extinguished by impossibility — narrow, **not** a general liability-exclusion-clause validity rule.
  - Art. 92: "Where a party's performance is made impossible for reasons beyond her/his control including the absence of the object matter of the contract or another case of force majeure, his/her obligation of performance shall be extinguished, unless circumstances indicate otherwise" — an express default rule, displaceable by "circumstances."
  - Art. 93-97: impossibility scenarios (death/incapacity, non-existence/deterioration of a necessary thing, new-regulation compliance, partial impossibility, disappearance of the object) — no mandatory notification/mitigation/suspension mechanics.
  - **No standalone "indemnity" article and no bright-line "fraud/gross-negligence exclusion clauses are void" rule exists anywhere in the statute's 165 articles** — the governing mechanism is the general Art. 58-61 public-order balancing doctrine, correcting the earlier secondary-sourced characterization.
- **Matrix rows affected:** Row 6 Rwanda (force majeure), row 8 Rwanda (liability cap), row 9 Rwanda (indemnity) — all **D -> C**, primary-text-verified. Rows 8-9 at **moderate confidence** (discretionary balancing doctrine, not a categorical rule); row 6 at higher confidence (explicit default/displaceable rule).
- **Residual uncertainty:** Moderate for rows 8-9 — Art. 58-61's case-specific discretion cannot certify every future application to the specific (not independently reviewed) SS19/SS20 clause wording; this finding confirms only that no *additional* mandatory statutory requirement beyond LEG-FD-15's existing carve-out was located.

## 5. Burundi Code Civil, Livre III (Des Contrats ou des Obligations Conventionnelles)

**Status: DIRECT PRIMARY-TEXT VERIFIED** (corrected under CORR-001 — originally PRIMARY SOURCE LOCATED BUT NOT SUFFICIENTLY VERIFIED; now read directly)

- **Jurisdiction:** Burundi
- **Official title:** Code Civil, Livre III — Des Contrats ou des Obligations Conventionnelles
- **Issuing authority / source:** Republic of Burundi; hosted by CEJP (Centre d'Etudes et de Prospective Juridique), an official/institutional Burundian legal-documentation body
- **Source URL:** `http://cejp.bi/sites/default/files/Code%20Civil%20Livre%20III.pdf`
- **Relevant articles read directly (confirmed via direct text extraction, not assumed from French numbering):**
  - Art. 45: the debtor is liable in damages for non-performance/delay unless the debtor shows the failure results from an external cause not attributable to the debtor, even absent bad faith.
  - Art. 46: "Il n'y a lieu a aucuns dommages-interets lorsque, par suite d'une force majeure ou d'un cas fortuit, le debiteur a ete empeche de donner ou de faire ce a quoi il etait oblige, ou a fait ce qui lui etait interdit." (No damages are owed where, due to force majeure or a fortuitous event, the debtor was prevented from giving or doing what he was obliged to do, or did what was forbidden to him.)
  - Art. 47: damages owed to a creditor are, generally, the loss suffered plus the gain of which the creditor was deprived, "sauf les exceptions et modifications ci-apres."
- **Matrix row affected:** Row 6 Burundi (force majeure). Result: **D -> C**, primary-text-verified, independently sourced from the Rwanda finding.
- **Residual uncertainty:** Moderate — Art. 45-47 themselves specify no notification/mitigation/suspension mechanics; this finding does not certify the undrafted SS25.4 clause's specific wording.
