## 1. Burundi — OHADA membership status

- **Jurisdiction:** Burundi
- **Question resolved:** Does an OHADA Uniform Act (arbitration, general commercial law, contract law for sale of goods) apply directly as Burundi domestic law, potentially overriding or restricting the Business Terms' Kigali/KIAC arbitration architecture?
- **Source type:** Primary/institutional (treaty organization's own site)
- **Source:** ohada.org — "States Members latest news," "General overview," and OHADA/OHADA.com coverage of Burundi's accession symposium
- **Article/instrument:** N/A (membership status, not a specific article)
- **Verified proposition:** As of September 2026, Burundi is not a ratified OHADA member state. 17 states are current members (Benin, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, CAR, Côte d'Ivoire, Congo, Comoros, Gabon, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Equatorial Guinea, Mali, Niger, DRC, Senegal, Chad, Togo) — Burundi is not among them. Accession discussions/symposia have occurred, but ratification (required to incorporate OHADA Uniform Acts into Burundi's domestic legal system) has not been confirmed complete.
- **Classification supported:** Row 1 (Burundi B2B forum) — removes OHADA Uniform Act override as a risk; does not itself resolve the row. Result: **D unchanged**.
- **Confidence / residual uncertainty:** Moderate-high for the membership fact itself (multiple institutional pages consistent); the underlying accession process is fluid and should be re-checked if this task's findings are relied on more than a few months out.

## 2. Burundi — Law No. 1/22 of 22 August 2024 (Code des Communications Électroniques et Postales)

- **Jurisdiction:** Burundi
- **Question resolved:** Does Burundi have a dedicated electronic-signature/e-contracting legal regime, and does it impose acceptance-mechanics requirements beyond the portable LEG-FD-03 standard?
- **Source type:** Primary (official regulator publication)
- **Source:** `https://arct.gov.bi/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/LOI_COMMUNICATIONS-ELECTRONIQUES.pdf` (Agence de Régulation et de Contrôle des Télécommunications, Burundi)
- **Article/instrument:** Titre I, Chapitre I (Art. 1–2, objet et champ d'application) and Chapitre II (Art. 3, définitions) — pages 1–8 of the PDF, read directly by this task
- **Verified proposition (direct read):** Art. 1: the law applies to "l'ensemble des transactions par voie électronique incluant notamment le commerce par voie électronique et le cyber-gouvernement." Art. 3 defines `authentification`, `certificat`, `certificat qualifié`, `non-répudiation`, and `commerce par voie électronique` — establishing that Burundi has a codified electronic-signature/authentication framework as of August 2024.
- **Verified proposition (secondary corroboration, Flowmono legal blog):** Qualified electronic signatures receive presumed authenticity; electronic and handwritten signatures are legally equivalent for commercial contracts (with standard exclusions: notarial acts, family law, certain mandated-handwritten instruments — none applicable to a B2B loyalty-platform Business Terms instrument). No specific forced-scrolling/retyping consent-mechanics requirement identified.
- **Classification supported:** Row 3 (Burundi e-contracting). Result: **D → C**.
- **Confidence / residual uncertainty:** High for the propositions actually read (Art. 1–3, scope/definitions). Not yet verified: the specific e-contract-formation/consent-mechanics articles later in the (large, ~100+ article) code, and reconciliation against the 2026 Personal Data Protection Law's "demonstrable consent" requirement cited by the existing External Legal Opinion. **Recommend a follow-up read of Law 1/22's substantive e-commerce chapters before this classification is relied on for actual clause drafting.**

## 3. Rwanda — Law N° 45/2011 of 25/11/2011 Governing Contracts

- **Jurisdiction:** Rwanda
- **Question resolved:** Does Rwandan mandatory law limit B2B liability-cap/indemnity-clause enforceability beyond what LEG-FD-15 already carves out?
- **Source type:** Secondary (institutional legal directory, Chambers and Partners "Commercial Contracts 2025 — Rwanda" Global Practice Guide), corroborating a primary source located but not directly read this task (official PDF at `minecofin.gov.rw`)
- **Article/instrument:** Article 92 (force majeure/impossibility of performance — cited by Chambers); general non-excludability principle for fraud/gross negligence/wilful misconduct (article number not confirmed by this task's secondary source)
- **Verified proposition:** "Any clause seeking to exclude liability for wilful misconduct, fraud or gross negligence is deemed void under Rwanda's Law N° 45/2011." Liability-limitation clauses are enforceable "if reasonable and negotiated transparently," subject to proportionality/essential-remedies review by courts or arbitral tribunals. Article 92: an obligation is extinguished where performance is prevented by the absence of the contract's object or other force majeure, "unless circumstances indicate otherwise."
- **Classification supported:** Row 8 Rwanda (liability cap) and row 9 Rwanda (indemnity) — both **D → C**, since LEG-FD-15's existing non-excludable-liability carve-out (fraud, wilful misconduct, gross negligence, death/personal injury, non-excludable statutory warranty) already matches this principle. Row 6 Rwanda (force majeure) — **D unchanged**, since Art. 92's existence does not by itself resolve whether the (undrafted) §25.4 clause needs jurisdiction-specific treatment.
- **Confidence / residual uncertainty:** Moderate — secondary-sourced, not this task's own primary-text read. The official primary PDF was located (`minecofin.gov.rw`) but not fetched/read within this task's research budget. **Recommend a follow-up direct read of Law 45/2011 (at minimum Art. 92 and the liability/exclusion-clause articles) before relying on this for actual clause drafting**, notwithstanding the C classification recorded here for the narrow "is this already consistent with LEG-FD-15" question.

## 4. Rwanda — Law n° 011/2026 of 26/02/2026 relating to Competition and Consumer Protection

- **Jurisdiction:** Rwanda
- **Question resolved:** Does Rwanda's new consumer-protection law impose B2B subscription/billing-disclosure or unfair-terms requirements on the Business Terms instrument?
- **Source type:** Secondary (KTPress, a Kigali-based news outlet reporting on the law's content; not a primary-text read)
- **Article/instrument:** Not cited to specific articles by this source
- **Verified proposition:** Law n° 011/2026 replaces the prior Law nº 36/2012 and modernizes Rwanda's competition/consumer-protection framework; its focus, per this source and per Chambers and Partners' separate commentary, is consumer (B2C) protection — B2B unfair-terms questions remain governed by Law 45/2011's general contract-law principles (see item 3), not this newer consumer statute.
- **Classification supported:** Row 8 Rwanda (narrows the candidate source pool, supporting the C classification above by ruling out this statute as an independent B2B liability-cap constraint); row 12 Rwanda (narrows but does not resolve — D unchanged, confirms this statute is not itself the missing B2B subscription-disclosure authority).
- **Confidence / residual uncertainty:** Moderate — single secondary news source; the law's own text was not read.

## 5. Burundi Code Civil, Livre III (Des Contrats ou des Obligations Conventionnelles)

- **Jurisdiction:** Burundi
- **Question resolved:** Does Burundi civil law recognize a general force-majeure/cas-fortuit exoneration doctrine relevant to the Business Terms' force-majeure clause?
- **Source type:** Primary, located but not read in full text this task
- **Source:** `http://cejp.bi/sites/default/files/Code%20Civil%20Livre%20III.pdf` (Centre d'Études et de Prospective Juridique, an official/institutional Burundian legal-documentation body)
- **Article/instrument:** Not identified to a specific article this task (French Code Civil tradition places this around Art. 1148, but Burundi's own article numbering was not confirmed)
- **Verified proposition:** Burundi's Code Civil follows the French civil-law tradition of force-majeure/cas-fortuit exoneration from contractual liability where performance is prevented by an irresistible, unforeseeable, external event — confirmed by general comparative civil-law search results, not by this task's own read of the Burundi-specific article text.
- **Classification supported:** Row 6 Burundi (force majeure) — **D unchanged**; existence of the general doctrine is background context, not a resolution of the specific clause-drafting question.
- **Confidence / residual uncertainty:** Low-moderate — the specific Burundi article was not read; only the general French-civil-law-derived doctrine's likely applicability was inferred and flagged as a residual verification item.

## 6. Rwanda — Law No 22/2018 of 29/04/2018 (Civil, Commercial, Labour and Administrative Procedure)

- **Jurisdiction:** Rwanda
- **Question resolved:** Does Rwandan procedural law impose contractual-notice-clause requirements, or only litigation/service-of-process requirements?
- **Source type:** Secondary (RwandaLII listing; a university civil-procedure course summary; a legal-practice LinkedIn article on Intent-to-Sue Letters), not a primary-text read
- **Article/instrument:** Not cited to specific articles this task
- **Verified proposition:** Law No 22/2018 governs court procedure, including a mandatory pre-filing amicable-settlement requirement and Intent-to-Sue Letter practice before litigation may be registered. This is litigation/service-of-process law — distinct from an ordinary contractual notice clause (deemed receipt, registered address, notice channel) in a Business Terms instrument.
- **Classification supported:** Row 4 Rwanda (notices) — **D unchanged**; confirms no evidence was found (or conflated) that Rwanda mandates a specific contractual-notice mechanism, and correctly keeps litigation service separate per the task's own instruction.
- **Confidence / residual uncertainty:** Moderate for the general procedural framework; the statute's own text was not read, and no search was performed specifically for contractual (non-litigation) notice-clause requirements — this remains a genuine gap, not a resolved "no requirement exists" finding.
