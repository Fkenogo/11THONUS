## DEC-LEGAL-006 — Proposed Update

**Corrected 2026-07-18 following direct review of Burundi's primary statutory text** (previously, Burundi's mechanism was entirely unconfirmed; it is now confirmed as an adequacy-based mechanism, narrowing but not closing the open items below).

- **Proposed status:** remains `OPEN_LEGAL` — **not proposed for CONFIRMED.** The evidence gathered (see [Legal Evidence Pack](DEC-LEGAL-006-cross-border-hosting-evidence-2026-07-18.md)) leaves material gaps — the contents of Burundi's Ministerial adequacy list and its new Agency's operational status; the practical Rwanda NCSA authorization pathway — that require counsel input this evidence-gathering pass could not supply. Proposing `CONFIRMED` now would be guessing a legal conclusion in place of counsel, which this evidence pack was explicitly instructed not to do.
- **Proposed decision (draft, for discussion, not adoption):** *"11thONUS, operated from Kigali, Rwanda, will host platform data outside Rwanda under Rwanda Law N° 058/2021 Articles 48–50's cross-border transfer mechanism (NCSA authorization and/or a documented Article 48 alternative ground, with an Article 49 written transfer contract), and under Burundi's Loi n° 1/03 du 10 mars 2026 Articles 15–16's adequacy-based mechanism (a Ministerial-list jurisdiction, or Agency-approved safeguards for a non-listed jurisdiction). The specific hosting jurisdiction is selected per DEC-TECH-005 from among jurisdictions confirmed legally admissible under this position."*
- **Evidence basis:** [Legal Evidence Pack](DEC-LEGAL-006-cross-border-hosting-evidence-2026-07-18.md) §2 (Rwanda), §3 (Burundi — corrected 2026-07-18 with confirmed primary-text findings), §5 (transfer-path analysis), §6 (language — corrected).
- **Safeguards proposed for inclusion once confirmed:** NCSA registration/authorization obtained before any production data leaves Rwanda; a written Article 49 transfer contract with Google/Firebase (or confirmation that Google's standard data-processing terms satisfy Article 49); on the Burundi side, either confirmation the hosting jurisdiction is on the Ministerial adequacy list or Agency-approved safeguards documentation; a Burundi-established representative if Article 2's territorial trigger and non-occasional-processing test are both met (Legal Evidence Pack §3); **direct confirmation of Firebase Authentication's actual data-residency position from Google's applicable service terms, data-processing documentation, or the provider directly, before either DEC-LEGAL-006 or DEC-TECH-005 is finally confirmed** — this evidence pack finds only that Authentication lacks a project-level location-selection control, not where Authentication data actually resides (Legal Evidence Pack §5, corrected).
- **Unresolved legal conditions (must be resolved before CONFIRMED is proposed again — narrowed 2026-07-18):**
  1. The contents of Burundi's Ministerial adequacy list (Loi n° 1/03, Art. 15 ¶3), if issued, or confirmation that Burundi's new Agency is operationally approving alternative safeguards (Art. 15 ¶4).
  2. The Agency's own implementing decree (missions/composition/organization, Art. 42 ¶2) — not located in this pass.
  3. Whether 11thONUS's Burundi-pilot technical stack triggers Article 2's local-representative requirement.
  4. Rwanda's realistic NCSA authorization pathway (cost, timeline, "legitimate interests" vs. full authorization) confirmed with NCSA or Rwandan counsel.
  5. Rwanda's Article 7/42 consent-language question resolved in practice (English/French sufficiency given Rwanda's now-confirmed four official languages — Kinyarwanda, English, French, Kiswahili) with Rwandan counsel.
  6. Whether Burundi's separate, pre-existing consumer-protection statute (Loi n° 1/11 de 2009) requires Kirundi for consumer notices — the data protection law itself was confirmed to contain no language requirement, but this separate statute was not re-reviewed in this pass.
- **Owner approval required:** Founder + qualified legal adviser (per DEC-LEGAL-006's existing Decision Register ownership — unchanged by this proposal).

## DEC-TECH-005 — Proposed Update

- **Proposed status:** remains `OPEN_ENGINEERING` — **not proposed for CONFIRMED.** No region can be responsibly finalized while DEC-LEGAL-006 remains open (this decision's own stated dependency) and while the latency/pricing gaps in the [Technical Evidence Pack](DEC-TECH-005-firebase-region-evaluation-2026-07-18.md) §4/§5 remain estimates rather than measurements.
- **Proposed region:** **none proposed for adoption.** The Technical Evidence Pack's advisory recommendation (`europe-west1` as the conservative first choice, `africa-south1` as the latency-optimized second choice) is carried forward for discussion only — see that pack's §9 for the full reasoning and explicit confidence caveat (rated "low-to-moderate").
- **Alternatives considered:** `africa-south1`, `europe-west1`, `europe-west3`, `europe-west4`, `europe-west9`, and the `eur3` multi-region option — see the [Technical Evidence Pack](DEC-TECH-005-firebase-region-evaluation-2026-07-18.md) §1 and §8 evidence table for the full comparison.
- **Recommendation basis:** service-availability facts carry high confidence (official Firebase/Google Cloud documentation); latency and pricing carry low confidence (estimates, not measurements — Technical Evidence Pack §4/§5); legal admissibility carries the same "potentially admissible, pending confirmation" status as every other candidate (Legal Evidence Pack §8).
- **Dependencies:** DEC-LEGAL-006 (must reach a resolvable state first, per the region's own decision question); an actual latency measurement from Kigali and Bujumbura (not yet obtained); confirmed per-region Firestore/Storage pricing (not yet independently obtained, distinct from the Functions/Run tiering evidence gathered).
- **Implementation implications (if this direction is eventually adopted, corrected 2026-07-18, second pass, to keep Cloud Scheduler and Cloud Tasks as separate open items):** if `africa-south1` is ultimately preferred, ENG-P1-001/ENG-P1-003 will need to (a) explicitly scope a manually-provisioned, cross-region Cloud Scheduler pattern (Technical Evidence Pack §2, §7 option 2) rather than assume Firebase's automatic `onSchedule` tooling will work as-is, **and separately** (b) resolve Cloud Tasks as its own unanswered architecture question — no equivalent cross-region pattern is confirmed for Cloud Tasks, so a queue in a supported region, an alternative mechanism, or a redesign must be evaluated independently (Technical Evidence Pack §2, §9 condition 6) before any Phase 1 requirement depending on Cloud Tasks can be scoped. If `europe-west1` is preferred, no mixed-region design is needed for either service.
- **Rollback or reconsideration trigger (proposed):** if actual latency measurements (once obtained) show `africa-south1`'s advantage over `europe-west1` is smaller than the directional estimate in this pack suggests, or if `africa-south1`'s pricing premium (once confirmed) is material, the conservative `europe-west1` recommendation should be treated as the default rather than revisited toward `africa-south1`.
